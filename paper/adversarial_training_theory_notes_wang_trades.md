# Notes: Wang et al. 2019 and TRADES/Zhang et al. 2019

## 1. Wang et al., "On the Convergence and Robustness of Adversarial Training"

Source: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/wang19i/wang19i.pdf

### What kind of paper is it?

This is mainly a theory plus empirical adversarial-training paper for image classification. It is not a PDE/operator-learning paper.

### One-sentence result

The quality of the inner adversarial maximization matters for final robust training, and if the inner attack is solved to a controlled first-order stationarity tolerance, the overall min-max adversarial training procedure has a convergence guarantee to a first-order stationary point up to an error term depending on that tolerance.

### Main idea

Adversarial training has an inner maximization step and an outer model-training step. The paper argues that not all generated adversarial examples are equally useful: what matters is not only the attack loss value, but whether the inner maximization has reached a good constrained stationary point.

They define a criterion called FOSC, first-order stationary condition, for the constrained inner maximization. Smaller FOSC means the adversarial example is closer to satisfying first-order optimality inside the perturbation set, so the inner maximization is solved better.

### Practical finding

The paper finds that very strong, highly converged adversarial examples are not always helpful at the beginning of training. In early training, weaker adversarial examples may help generalization. Later in training, stronger and better-converged adversarial examples become important for robustness.

This motivates Dynamic Adversarial Training: gradually decrease the allowed FOSC threshold during training, so early attacks are weaker and later attacks are stronger.

### Assumptions for the theory

The convergence theorem assumes:

- smoothness / gradient Lipschitz conditions for the loss with respect to model parameters and input;
- local strong concavity of the inner maximization problem in the perturbation set;
- bounded variance of stochastic gradients;
- the inner maximizer is approximate in the FOSC sense.

### What the theorem says

Under these assumptions, the average squared norm of the gradient of the outer robust objective decreases at a sublinear rate, up to an additional error term controlled by the inner-maximization tolerance. In plain language: if the inner attack is accurate enough, the whole adversarial-training min-max procedure can converge to an approximate stationary point.

### Meaning for our paper

This supports the sentence that inner attack quality matters. For our setting, it does not directly prove convergence of solver-integrated PDE adversarial training, because our losses involve PDE solver outputs, regression/operator learning, and sometimes solver gradients. But it is useful background: attack quality and attack schedule affect the outer training result.

## 2. Zhang et al., "Theoretically Principled Trade-off between Robustness and Accuracy" / TRADES

Source: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/zhang19p/zhang19p.pdf

### What kind of paper is it?

This is a theory plus algorithm plus experiment paper for adversarial robustness in classification. It is not a PDE/operator-learning paper.

### One-sentence result

Robust classification error can be decomposed into natural classification error plus boundary error, and this leads to a tight surrogate upper bound that motivates the TRADES objective for controlling the robustness-accuracy trade-off.

### Main idea

The paper defines:

- natural error: the ordinary classification error on clean inputs;
- robust error: the error after allowing an adversarial perturbation within an epsilon ball;
- boundary error: the probability that a correctly classified sample lies close enough to the decision boundary that a small perturbation can cross it.

The key decomposition is:

```text
robust error = natural error + boundary error
```

This says robustness is not only about fitting labels; it is also about pushing the decision boundary away from the data.

### TRADES objective

The theory motivates an objective with two parts:

- clean supervised loss for accuracy;
- consistency regularization between the prediction on a clean input and the prediction on an adversarially perturbed input for robustness.

In implementation, the second term is usually a KL-divergence-style loss between model outputs at clean and adversarial inputs, and the adversarial input is generated by an inner PGD-like maximization.

### Assumptions for the theory

The main theoretical bound is developed mostly for binary classification. It assumes a classification-calibrated surrogate loss, meaning minimizing the surrogate is consistent with minimizing classification error. The lower-bound/tightness result also assumes mild extra conditions on the surrogate loss.

The algorithmic multiclass version is a practical extension using multiclass calibrated losses such as cross entropy.

### What the theorem says

Theorem 3.1 gives an upper bound on robust error minus optimal natural error. The bound contains:

- a surrogate natural-risk term;
- a term measuring whether predictions change inside the input perturbation ball, which controls boundary error.

Theorem 3.2 says this style of upper bound is essentially tight in a worst-case sense under extra loss conditions.

### Meaning for our paper

TRADES supports the idea that adversarial training is naturally a trade-off: improving robustness usually comes with a clean-accuracy cost, and a principled objective should separate clean fitting from perturbation consistency.

For our PDE operator setting, the classification label is replaced by solver output or physics residual. TRADES does not prove our method, but it gives a useful analogy: one term fits the reference behavior, while another term controls sensitivity around perturbed inputs.

## Quick comparison

| Paper | Main concern | Theory target | Practical message |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wang et al. 2019 | Inner maximization quality in adversarial training | Min-max convergence under FOSC-controlled approximate inner attacks | Attack strength should be scheduled; later attacks need better convergence |
| Zhang et al. 2019 / TRADES | Accuracy-robustness trade-off | Robust error decomposition and tight surrogate bound | Robustness needs boundary/consistency control, not only label fitting |

## How to use them in our paper

The safest way to cite them is as background, not as direct guarantees for PDE operator learning.

- Use Wang et al. to justify: the quality of the inner attack affects the quality of adversarial training.
- Use TRADES to justify: adversarial training naturally involves a robustness-accuracy trade-off and can be interpreted through clean fitting plus perturbation consistency.
- Do not claim either paper proves convergence or robustness for solver-integrated PDE operator training directly.
